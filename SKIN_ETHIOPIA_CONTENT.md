## The skin in one breath

Chad is in Addis Ababa. He thinks he's rescuing a woman named Tigist. Tigist is a software engineer at a buna bet, drinking her third macchiato, wondering why this man in cargo shorts just texted her "omw to save u." She texts back in a mix of English and Amharic. Ge'ez script appears in her messages. The player doesn't know what the characters mean. Then they're in the level — a sun-baked street lined with market stalls — surrounded by objects. They grab the ones they think match the words. They reach the end. They find out if they were right.

The joke is always Chad. Ethiopia is treated with warmth, specificity, and the kind of detail that makes someone from Addis nod and say "yeah, that's right."

## The cast

**Chad** — same Chad. Cargo shorts, Hawaiian shirt, fanny pack, baseball cap. He flew to Addis Ababa because he saw a documentary about the Ark of the Covenant and thought it needed protecting. He cannot pronounce a single Amharic word correctly. He tried to order "buna" and said "tuna." He is the joke. Always.

**Tigist** (ትግስት — "patience") — Amharic-speaking software engineer. She's at Tomoca, the legendary coffee house in Addis. She's on her laptop. She did not ask for this. She texts Chad in a mix of English and Amharic because she's multitasking and keeps slipping into her own language out of habit, not to teach him. She's warmer than Anya underneath the exasperation — there's an aunty energy, a "I'm going to feed you because that's what we do even though you don't deserve it" quality. She is smarter than Chad, more competent than Chad, and increasingly amused by Chad in a way she'd never admit.

**An Ethiopian grandmother reading Tigist's lines should see:** a younger version of herself — the woman who held everything together while the overdressed, underprepared man kept showing up at her door with the wrong thing from the market. And she let him keep coming back. The game never says this. But it's there, in the warmth under the sharpness.

**The Aunties** — patrol the ground carrying overloaded Merkato shopping bags. Walk with purpose and authority. On contact they don't hurt Chad — they shove him backward, tell him he's too skinny (untranslated Amharic text flash: "ቀጭን ነህ!" — "You're skinny!"), and keep moving. They're not enemies. They're concerned.

**The Blue Taxis** — Addis Ababa's blue-and-white minibus taxis. They rip across the screen honking. They will kill you. The horn sound is implied by a screen-edge "BEEP" text flash as they enter. They do not slow down. They have never slowed down. This is known.

**The Sacred Coffee Bean** — it has a face. Two ancient, knowing eyes. A serene, patient expression. It sits on a high platform emanating a warm golden glow. It is the original bean. The one that a goat herder's goat ate in the Kaffa region centuries ago, starting a global addiction. It feels no guilt. It has seen empires rise and fall. It is worth 100 points. It watches.

## Environment

**Visual setting:** A sun-warmed street in Addis Ababa. Warm earth tones — terracotta, sand, dusty gold. Market stalls with corrugated tin awnings in faded blues and greens. Cobblestone ground. In the background, eucalyptus trees and the suggestion of hills. The sky is bright blue with high white clouds — Addis is at 2,400 meters elevation, the light is clean and bright, not tropical haze.

**Ground tile:** Warm tan cobblestone with visible stone grid pattern. Different feel from Belarus's dark green grass.

**Platform tile:** Stacked wood and corrugated tin — market stall shelving. Warm brown wood with blue-gray tin patches.

**Gate building:** Tomoca Coffee House — a modest, warm-colored building with a hand-painted sign reading "ቡና ቤት" (buna bet — coffee house). Wooden door, steamy window, warm light inside. This is where Tigist is. This is where the groceries go.

**Background:** Light blue sky, distant green hills (Entoto Mountains), eucalyptus tree silhouettes. Warm, bright, high-altitude African city — not the safari/desert that Western media defaults to.

**Color palette shift from Belarus:**
- Belarus: dark navy sky, cool greens, concrete grays — Eastern European winter dusk
- Ethiopia: warm blue sky, terracotta, dusty golds, rich browns — high-altitude sunlight

## Language notes

**Amharic (አማርኛ)** uses Ge'ez script (ፊደል — fidel). Each character represents a consonant-vowel combination (a syllabary, not an alphabet). This is fundamentally different from Cyrillic — the player isn't just learning new letters, they're learning that a single character can carry a whole syllable.

For Level 1, we start simple: common food words that are short (2-3 Ge'ez characters) and visually distinctive. The player won't learn the whole fidel system from a game. But they'll learn that ቡና means coffee and ዳቦ means bread, and they'll learn it the way babies learn — by context, repetition, and getting it wrong.

**Transliteration note for Mom:** Amharic words in Tigist's messages should render in Ge'ez script, not Latin transliteration. The whole point is that the player sees unfamiliar characters and infers meaning from context. If we transliterate, we defeat the mechanic.

The obvious starting point. Buna (coffee) is to Ethiopia what ПРОДУКТЫ is to Belarus — foundational. Chad's first errand is getting coffee supplies for Tigist, who is out of beans at the worst possible time.

| sukwar | ስኳር | sugar | sukwar_bag |

### Briefing messages (Tigist)

**dabo_round** — Ethiopian bread. Round, golden-brown, slightly domed. Distinct from any other bread in the game — it's round, not loaf-shaped.

**sukwar_bag** — white sugar bag, similar shape to the Belarus sugar but with Amharic text "ስኳር" on the label.

**teff_sack** — a small sack of teff grain. Tiny brown/tan grains. The player might mistake this for coffee beans, but teff is much lighter and finer.

**mango** — a ripe mango. Yellow-orange. Obviously fruit, not coffee supplies.

**bottled_water** — a plastic water bottle. Clear with blue label. Generic, clearly not what Tigist asked for.

### Reveal lines

**ቡና correct:** "You brought ቡና. Real ቡና. Maybe there's hope for you yet. ...don't smile at me like that."

**ቡና wrong:** "You came to Ethiopia — the country that INVENTED coffee — and you couldn't find ቡና. I need a moment."

**ወተት correct:** "ወተት. Fresh milk. From the corner shop. You actually listened. That's... unusual."

**ወተት wrong:** "No ወተት. No milk. You know buna without milk is like... actually, I take mine black. But that's not the point."

**ዳቦ correct:** "ዳቦ. The round one. Good. We'll eat. Even you."

**ዳቦ wrong:** "Where is the ዳቦ? Bread, Chad. Round. Golden. It was right there. You walked past bread."

**ስኳር correct:** "ስኳር. Sugar. Don't tell anyone about the two spoons."

**ስኳር wrong:** "No ስኳር? How am I supposed to drink my ቡና? ...don't say 'black.' Don't."

### Gate fail

**"ቡናዬ የት ነው?!"** (Where is my coffee?!) — not as cosmic as Belarus's screaming. More personal. More offended. Tigist's voice, not the universe's.

## Level 3: "The Dinner"

Tigist is cooking. This is serious. Ethiopian dinner is a communal ritual — injera spread on a shared plate, stews arranged on top, everyone eats with their hands from the same plate. Chad doesn't know this. He's about to learn.

### Vocab pack

| Word | Amharic | Meaning | Matches Item |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| injera | እንጀራ | sourdough flatbread | injera_roll |
| miser | ምስር | red lentils | miser_bag |
| qey_sir | ቀይ ስር | beetroot | qey_sir_beet |
| karya | ቃሪያ | hot green pepper | karya_pepper |

### Briefing messages

```
1. "Tonight you eat like an Ethiopian. First: እንጀራ. Don't call it a pancake."
2. "I need ምስር. Red ones. For the wot."
3. "ቀይ ስር. Don't panic, it's just beetroot. It turns everything pink. That's normal."
4. "And ቃሪያ. Green, small, very hot. If you bite one I am not responsible."
5. "We eat from the same plate. With our hands. RIGHT hand only. We'll discuss."
```

### Collectible items — targets

**injera_roll** — rolled gray-tan sourdough flatbread. Spongy texture visible. (Was a decoy in Level 2, now a target. Cross-level learning again.)

**miser_bag** — small bag of red/orange lentils. Tiny round grains, warm red-orange color.

**qey_sir_beet** — a beetroot. Dark red-purple, round, with a small green leaf tuft on top.

**karya_pepper** — small green hot pepper. Bright green, elongated.

### Collectible items — decoys

**timatim_tomato** — tomato (from Level 2 — now it's a decoy)
**sukwar_bag** — sugar (from Level 1)
**mango** — mango (recurring decoy)

### Reveal lines

**እንጀራ correct:** "እንጀራ. You brought it. Did you call it a pancake in your head? You did. I can tell."

**እንጀራ wrong:** "No እንጀራ. That's like coming to dinner without the table, Chad. እንጀራ IS the table."

**ምስር correct:** "ምስር. Red lentils. The wot will be good tonight. I'll make enough for you too. I suppose."

**ምስር wrong:** "Without ምስር there is no wot. Without wot there is no dinner. You've just cancelled dinner."

**ቀይ ስር correct:** "ቀይ ስር. Beetroot. Everything will be pink now. Your fingers, the እንጀራ, everything. You'll survive."

**ቀይ ስር wrong:** "You didn't bring ቀይ ስር. It's the pink one. The very obviously pink one. How."

**ቃሪያ correct:** "ቃሪያ. Do NOT bite into it raw. I'm warning you. ...he's going to bite into it."

### Gate fail

**"እራት የለም።"** (There is no dinner.) — flat, declarative, final. Not angry. Just a fact. The Amharic equivalent of Level 4 Belarus's quiet devastation, but delivered earlier because Ethiopian hospitality makes the failure of a meal more personal.

## Level 4: "Buna Ceremony"

The coffee ceremony. This is sacred. Three rounds of coffee, prepared from raw beans roasted in front of you, ground by hand, brewed in a jebena. It takes over an hour. It is not Starbucks. Chad is about to sit through the most important ritual in Ethiopian social life and he has no idea.

### Vocab pack

| Word | Amharic | Meaning | Matches Item |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| jebena | ጀበና | traditional coffee pot | jebena_pot |
| rekbot | ርኩብ | small coffee cup (without handle) | rekbot_cup |
| itan | ዕጣን | incense (frankincense) | itan_incense |
| kolo | ቆሎ | roasted barley snack | kolo_bowl |

### Briefing messages

```
1. "We're doing ቡና ceremony. This is not optional."
2. "I need the ጀበና. The clay one with the straw lid. If you bring a French press I will end this."
3. "And ርኩብ. The small cups. No handles. They're not broken. They were made that way."
4. "Get ዕጣን from the woman by the church. You'll smell it before you see her."
5. "And ቆሎ. Roasted barley. You eat it with the coffee. Don't ask for biscotti."
6. "Three rounds, Chad. Three. This takes time. Sit. Be still. ...can you be still?"
```

### Collectible items — targets

**jebena_pot** — traditional Ethiopian clay coffee pot. Round-bottomed black clay with a narrow neck and straw lid/stopper. Distinctive shape — nothing else looks like a jebena.

**rekbot_cup** — tiny handleless coffee cup. Small, ceramic/clay, no handle (this is important — the lack of handle is the visual clue that separates it from any Western cup).

**itan_incense** — bundle/brick of frankincense. Pale amber/white crystalline chunks in a small clay dish or wrapped in paper.

**kolo_bowl** — a small bowl of roasted barley. Brown/golden toasted grains in a round bowl.

### Collectible items — decoys

**bottled_water** — water bottle (recurring)
**honey_jar** — honey jar (from Level 2 decoy pool)
**dabo_round** — bread (from Level 1 — not part of coffee ceremony)
**tea_cup** — a Western-style teacup WITH a handle. The handle is the tell — ርኩብ has no handle.

### Reveal lines

**ጀበና correct:** "The ጀበና. You found it. Do you know how old this design is? Older than your country. Much older."

**ጀበና wrong:** "You came to a buna ceremony without a ጀበና. That's like coming to a baptism without water."

**ርኩብ correct:** "ርኩብ. No handle. That confused you, didn't it? You kept looking for a handle. There is no handle. This is fine."

**ርኩብ wrong:** "Did you bring a cup with a HANDLE? That's not ርኩብ. We don't need handles. We have hands."

**ዕጣን correct:** "ዕጣን. Incense. Close your eyes. Breathe. ...keep your eyes closed a little longer. I'm making a face."

**ዕጣን wrong:** "No ዕጣን? No incense? The ceremony needs it. The room needs to smell right or it's just... drinking coffee. And it's not just drinking coffee."

**ቆሎ correct:** "ቆሎ. Roasted barley. Now we have everything. Now we can begin. ...I'm glad you're here. Don't read into that."

**ቆሎ wrong:** "No ቆሎ. What were you planning to eat during three rounds of coffee? Nothing? Just sitting there? ...actually that might be worse."

### Gate fail

**"ይሂዱ።"** (Go.) — one word. Formal register ("you" as ይሂዱ not ሂድ). She's not even angry. She's using the polite form. That's how disappointed she is. She's being formal with someone she was about to do a coffee ceremony with. In Ethiopian culture, that distance is devastating.

## Cross-level pedagogy notes

Same mechanic as Belarus — items rotate between target and decoy across levels:
- **berbere_tin**: decoy in L1, target in L2
- **injera_roll**: decoy in L2, target in L3
- **buna_bag**: target in L1, part of the world in L4 (the whole level is about coffee ceremony)

This means by Level 4, the player has seen ቡና (coffee) as a target, as context, and as ceremony. They don't just know the word — they know the culture around it. Three levels of exposure before the ceremony level hits. That's not accident. That's curriculum design wearing a comedy hat.

## The two rules, applied

1. **Is it funny?** Chad in Addis Ababa, trying to buy frankincense from a woman by a church, carrying it in his fanny pack. Chad biting into a raw ቃሪያ and crying. Chad looking for a handle on a ርኩብ. Chad being told he's too skinny by three aunties simultaneously. Yes. It's funny.

2. **Did the player learn a word?** ቡና, ወተት, ዳቦ, ስኳር, ሽሮ, በርበሬ, ቅቤ, ቲማቲም, እንጀራ, ምስር, ቀይ ስር, ቃሪያ, ጀበና, ርኩብ, ዕጣን, ቆሎ. Sixteen Amharic words across four levels. The player can walk into an Ethiopian restaurant and read part of the menu. They didn't study. They played a game about a man in cargo shorts. The coffee bean watched.
